Verification rule

“Don't promote an asset to Verified because one field looks right.”

Verified means the location, identity, and ownership/evidence trail agree. A stale record can be informative without being authoritative.

THE FRAMEWORK

Three levels of confidence

The goal isn't a perfect spreadsheet. The goal is a defensible understanding of the environment.

01

Verified

We know the asset exists, where it is, and the supporting information is current.

  • Recent telemetry or physical verification
  • Serial / hostname matches
  • Owner or custodian confirmed
02

Probable

We have strong evidence, but something is stale, indirect, or awaiting confirmation.

  • Recent-but-not-current telemetry
  • Conflicting metadata
  • Ownership inferred from records
03

Unknown

We cannot confidently establish the asset's current location, identity, or custody.

  • No recent check-in
  • Conflicting or missing records
  • Requires investigation

How I would answer the interview question

  1. Normalize the inventory. Establish one canonical asset ID and reconcile duplicates across systems.
  2. Collect evidence. Compare CMDB records to endpoint telemetry, network discovery, procurement, assignment records, and physical audits.
  3. Score confidence. Put every asset into Verified, Probable, or Unknown based on evidence quality—not intuition.
  4. Prioritize remediation. Focus first on unknown assets with high security, financial, or operational impact.
  5. Close the loop. Verification should update the source of truth and create an audit trail so the same problem doesn't recur.
